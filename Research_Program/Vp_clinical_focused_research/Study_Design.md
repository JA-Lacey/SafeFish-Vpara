## Regional endemicity of _V. parahaemolyticus_ lineages associated with foodborne outbreaks in Australia and New Zealand 
 
This project invoved setting up a coordinated national surveillance project drawing contributions from almost all Australian states and territories has been established to systematically collect and sequence _V. parahaemolyticus_ from clinical cases, oysters, seafood products, and diverse aquatic environments. 

By integrating WGS data from Australian isolates with publicly available sequences from international outbreaks (including New Zealand, which has similarly reported ST36 and ST50 seafood‐associated incidents)13, we aim to contextualize the genomic diversity and transmission dynamics of outbreak-associated sequence types.

This study analysed 676 _Vibrio parahaemolyticus_ genomes collected from around Australia between 2004 and 2025 from clinical cases (n=363), environmental/food sources (n=299), and unknown sources (n=14). Most isolates were collected around the outbreak years (2019–2024, n=600). Seven jurisdictions contributed data; all but one provided both clinical and environmental/foods isoaltes. 

We identifted 83 unique MLST combinations, with an additional 100 isolates with novel MLST profiles. Capsular typing revealed 17 O-serotypes and 75 K-serotypes. Clinical isolates were predominantly from gastroenteritis/faecal samples. Environmental/food isolates were from targeted sampling of oysters (n=249), with smaller numbers from other seafood (prawns, snails, mussels; n=20), miscellaneous foods (n=25), and water samples (n=4). 

We analysed an additional 10,437 publicly available _V. parahaemolyticus_ genomes (1,419 MLSTs/1,063 poppunk groups; 1951–2024) to assess the global prevalence of the key toxigenic clones. These included ST50 (n=76), ST417 (n=66), ST36 (n=801), ST3 (n=2,812), ST65 (n=13), ST817 (n=6) and ST1140 (n=10). Apart from ST3 and ST36, most toxigenic clones of interest in Oceania are rarely reported and found at lower prevalences. ST3 and ST36 dominate the genomes globally
